ACT I · THE HISTORICAL CHRONICLE

From Beowulf to Indian English.

Language evolves across generations. Experience the four monumental eras of English with original recordings and side-by-side translations.

c. 450 – 1100 AD

Old English

Germanic dialects of Angles, Saxons and Jutes, infused with Old Norse. Highly inflected with flexible syntax.

“Hwæt! We Gar-Dena in gear-dagum, þeod-cyninga, þrym gefrunon...”
Beowulf: “Listen! We have heard of the glory of the Spear-Danes in days of yore...”
c. 1100 – 1500 AD

Middle English

Norman Conquest of 1066 brought thousands of French and Latin terms. Inflections simplified, rhythm flourished.

“Whan that Aprill with his shoures soote the droghte of March hath perced to the roote...”
Chaucer’s Canterbury Tales: “When April with its sweet showers has pierced March’s drought...”
c. 1500 – 1800 AD

Early Modern

The Great Vowel Shift, Caxton's press in 1476, Shakespeare’s 1,700+ coined idioms and the King James Bible.

“Shall I compare thee to a summer's day? Thou art more lovely and more temperate...”
Shakespeare Sonnet 18: Celebrating eternal beauty through the poetic power of verse.
c. 1800 – TODAY

Indian & Global English

Science, tech, law and the vibrant cadence of Indian English. The language of higher education, courts, and global literature.

“Words are the catalysts of democracy and modern discovery in our interconnected world.”
Indian English: Vibrant literature from R.K. Narayan to modern tech journalism and global law.

Explore a few high-impact English sound contrasts with a visual mouth-placement guide and optional model audio. The aim is intelligibility—not copying somebody else's accent.

MOUTH PLACEMENT
Illustrative guide · not a medical model

/θ/

TH as in think

Let the tongue tip appear just between the teeth and send a small stream of air through the gap. Keep the sound unvoiced.

Keep your jaw relaxed. Do not clamp your teeth together or force the tongue forward.

thinkthreethoughthealthy

Try: think / sink · three / tree

English pronunciation varies across regions and speakers. No accent is automatically better; work toward being understood by the people you need to communicate with.
