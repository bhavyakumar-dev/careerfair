c. 450 – 1100 AD

Old English

Germanic dialects brought to Britain by the Angles, Saxons and Jutes, heavily shaped by Old Norse after Viking settlement. Grammar was highly inflected, much like modern German, with flexible word order.

Notable work: Beowulf, an epic poem.

c. 1100 – 1500 AD

Middle English

After the Norman Conquest of 1066, Anglo-Norman French became the language of royalty and law, and thousands of French and Latin words entered English. Grammar simplified and complex noun endings faded.

Notable work: Chaucer’s The Canterbury Tales.

c. 1500 – 1800 AD

Early Modern English

The Great Vowel Shift changed pronunciation between roughly 1400 and 1700. Caxton brought the printing press to England in 1476, standardising spelling and grammar. Shakespeare coined over 1,700 words, and the King James Bible enriched the vocabulary.

c. 1800 – today

Late Modern English

The Industrial and Scientific Age demanded thousands of new terms from Greek and Latin roots. Colonial history and trade carried English around the world, giving rise to Indian, American and Australian English.
